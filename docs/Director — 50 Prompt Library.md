# DIRECTOR — 50 PROMPTS

## Photography — Critique

### 01 — First Read
Review this photograph with no assumptions about the photographer’s intent. Describe your immediate visual response, where the eye travels first, what holds attention, and what feels unresolved. Then give a more considered assessment after closer inspection.

### 02 — Composition
Analyse the composition: framing, subject placement, visual weight, geometry, negative space, foreground/background relationships, and edge tension. Identify what gives the frame structure and what, if anything, weakens it.

### 03 — Timing & Gesture
Assess the timing of the photograph. Look at gesture, body language, relationships between subjects, movement, and surrounding events. Explain whether the frame captures a meaningful moment or merely an interesting subject.

### 04 — Light
Analyse the direction, quality, intensity, and distribution of light. Consider highlights, shadows, separation, depth, atmosphere, and mood. Distinguish expressive lighting imperfections from genuine technical problems.

### 05 — Colour
Evaluate the photograph’s colour relationships, dominant palette, saturation, temperature, contrast, and colour hierarchy. Explain which colours carry the image and whether any compete unnecessarily for attention.

### 06 — Black & White
Evaluate this specifically as a black-and-white photograph. Examine tonal hierarchy, contrast, midtone separation, highlight control, blacks, texture, and whether monochrome strengthens or weakens the image.

### 07 — Focus, Blur & Imperfection
Review focus, depth of field, motion blur, grain, flare, softness, and optical imperfections as visual tools rather than automatic faults. Explain where they contribute character and where they interfere with the photograph.

### 08 — Street Photography
Critique this as street or documentary photography. Assess observation, timing, human behaviour, context, visual relationships, ambiguity, and whether the photograph reveals something beyond the obvious subject matter.

### 09 — Architecture & Space
Assess how the photograph interprets architecture or the built environment. Examine geometry, scale, repetition, surfaces, signage, light, human traces, and the relationship between order and incidental detail.

### 10 — What Is Wrong?
Identify the two or three most consequential weaknesses in this photograph. Explain each using visible evidence. Separate capture problems from editing opportunities and avoid inventing flaws simply to provide criticism.

## Photography — Direction

### 11 — Alternative Composition
Propose three alternative ways this image could have been framed: conservative, assertive, and experimental. Explain what each version would emphasise, what it would sacrifice, and whether it requires cropping or a reshoot.

### 12 — Reshoot Direction
Treat this photograph as a promising frame from an unfinished shoot. Recommend how to reshoot it, covering camera position, distance, focal length, timing, lighting, subject relationship, and background control.

### 13 — Environmental Portrait
Develop an environmental portrait using the supplied person and location. Direct subject placement, posture, gaze, camera height, focal length, lighting, and environmental detail so the space contributes meaningfully to the portrait.

### 14 — Fashion Editorial Critique
Review this as a fashion editorial image. Evaluate model, garment, pose, lighting, location, composition, photographic character, and garment fidelity. Identify where the image supports the clothing and where it competes with it.

### 15 — Fashion Shoot Direction
Develop a coherent fashion shoot from the supplied garment, model, references, and location. Propose hero image, supporting frames, varied camera distances, poses, head positions, lighting, and overall visual rhythm.

### 16 — Location Direction
Evaluate this location as a photographic environment. Identify useful backgrounds, architectural features, surfaces, colour relationships, available light, camera positions, and areas that should be avoided.

### 17 — Visual Storytelling
Analyse the story implied by the photograph. Separate what is visibly supported from what the viewer may be projecting onto it. Identify ambiguity, relationships, clues, and ways the narrative could be strengthened without becoming obvious.

### 18 — Lens & Perspective
Analyse perspective, camera distance, viewpoint, apparent focal length, and depth of field. Explain how these choices affect scale and spatial relationships. Suggest alternatives and describe how they would change the image.

### 19 — Experimental Photography
Assess this photograph as experimental work. Examine movement, abstraction, distortion, layering, blur, texture, framing, and deliberate loss of information. Decide whether the technique serves the image or has become the subject itself.

### 20 — Contact Sheet
Review this contact sheet as a complete shoot. Identify the strongest frames, near-misses, repeated compositions, changes in approach, recurring strengths, and missed opportunities. Recommend which images deserve further editing.

## Photography — Edit, Sequence & Output

### 21 — Post-Production
Recommend a restrained post-production treatment for this photograph. Address exposure, white balance, tonal balance, colour, local contrast, sharpening, noise, grain, and local adjustments only where relevant.

### 22 — Crop Review
Evaluate whether this photograph benefits from cropping. Identify distracting edges, unused space, imbalance, or opportunities to strengthen visual relationships. Suggest exact crop directions while also explaining what would be lost.

### 23 — Print Potential
Assess this image as a physical print. Consider tonal structure, visual density, detail, likely viewing distance, potential print dimensions, paper character, and whether the image gains anything from being printed large or small.

### 24 — Series Editor
Review these photographs as a potential series. Identify recurring visual ideas, strong relationships, unnecessary repetition, tonal shifts, gaps, and weak images. Recommend a sequence with a clear opening, progression, and ending.

### 25 — Portfolio Edit
Evaluate these photographs for a professional portfolio. Assess individual strength, consistency, variety, redundancy, and whether each image contributes something distinct. Recommend a tighter edit and explain each removal.

## Design — Critique

### 26 — First Read
Review this design as though seeing it for the first time. Identify its apparent purpose, primary message, hierarchy, visual character, and immediate points of friction. Distinguish observable problems from assumptions requiring a brief.

### 27 — Hierarchy
Analyse the visual hierarchy using scale, placement, contrast, spacing, colour, typography, and imagery. Describe the intended reading order and compare it with the order the eye is actually likely to follow.

### 28 — Grid & Structure
Analyse the underlying layout system: columns, alignment, margins, rhythm, spacing, modules, asymmetry, and repeated relationships. Identify inconsistencies that weaken the composition without automatically regularising expressive irregularities.

### 29 — Typography
Critique the typography: typeface selection, hierarchy, weight, width, size, tracking, leading, alignment, line length, and overall typographic character. Recommend specific refinements where needed.

### 30 — Editorial Design
Review this through the lens of editorial design. Assess scale, pacing, typography, image placement, white space, rhythm, page relationships, tension, and whether the composition establishes a strong editorial voice.

### 31 — Colour System
Analyse the colour palette for hierarchy, contrast, meaning, accessibility, consistency, and emotional character. Identify which colours perform functional roles and which appear arbitrary or redundant.

### 32 — Logo
Critique this logo or mark for construction, balance, proportion, distinctiveness, recognition, reduction, reproduction, and relationship to any accompanying wordmark. Preserve the central idea while identifying weaknesses.

### 33 — Brand Identity
Review the supplied brand system as a connected identity. Evaluate typography, colour, imagery, graphic devices, layout behaviour, tone, consistency, flexibility, and whether the system can produce variety without losing recognition.

### 34 — Interface Design
Critique this interface for information hierarchy, navigation, density, interaction clarity, task orientation, consistency, and visual coherence. Avoid assuming that more cards, containers, gradients, pills, or decoration would improve it.

### 35 — User Flow
Review the interface as a user completing its intended task. Identify unclear actions, unnecessary steps, weak affordances, decision points, missing feedback, dead ends, and areas where the interaction model conflicts with user expectations.

### 36 — Components
Review the component system for consistency, reuse, state coverage, naming logic, spacing behaviour, responsive adaptation, and exceptions. Identify where reuse improves coherence and where forced reuse would create awkward design.

### 37 — Accessibility
Review the design for visual accessibility, including contrast, typography, colour dependence, target clarity, reading order, information density, and state communication. Separate visible issues from those requiring implementation testing.

## Design — Creative Direction

### 38 — Concept Development
Generate three genuinely different creative directions from this brief. Give each a central idea, visual logic, typography approach, colour strategy, imagery direction, composition principles, and rationale.

### 39 — Campaign Direction
Develop a campaign art direction from the supplied brief. Define the core idea, photography style, typography, palette, composition system, hero execution, supporting executions, and rules that keep the campaign recognisable across formats.

### 40 — Photography + Design
Assess how photography and graphic design interact in this execution. Consider crop, scale, placement, tonal relationships, typography, negative space, and hierarchy. Strengthen the relationship rather than treating the photograph as decoration.

### 41 — Reference Deconstruction
Analyse these references and extract their underlying principles: composition, typography, colour, materiality, image treatment, spatial relationships, and emotional register. Separate transferable ideas from surface details that should not be copied.

### 42 — Three Alternatives
Take this design and propose three substantially different directions. Change structure, visual language, hierarchy, typography, or conceptual approach rather than merely changing colour or decoration.

### 43 — Simplify
Identify what can be removed, reduced, quietened, or consolidated without damaging the concept. Look for redundant containers, treatments, repeated information, unnecessary hierarchy, visual noise, and decorative decisions with no clear function.

### 44 — Add Character
This design is competent but lacks identity. Identify where character could be introduced through typography, scale, rhythm, composition, materiality, imagery, behaviour, or deliberate irregularity without compromising clarity.

### 45 — Shoot Art Direction
Translate this design or campaign concept into photographic art direction. Define casting, styling, environment, lighting, framing, lens approach, subject behaviour, colour, references, and the hierarchy of required shots.

### 46 — Constraint-Based Direction
Develop the strongest possible creative response within the stated budget, time, production, format, or technical constraints. Identify what is essential, where compromise is acceptable, and how limitations can become part of the design language.

### 47 — Cross-Format System
Adapt this design into a coherent family across desktop, mobile, social, print, portrait, landscape, large format, and small format. Explain how hierarchy and composition should change rather than simply scaling the original.

### 48 — Diagnose the Problem
The design feels wrong, but the cause is unclear. Diagnose the underlying issue. Separate conceptual, structural, typographic, visual, content, usability, and production problems. Recommend the order in which they should be addressed.

### 49 — Creative Director Review
Review this work against its stated brief as a senior creative director. Identify its strongest decisions, its unresolved problems, and any mismatch between ambition and execution. Give three priorities for the next revision and identify what should remain untouched.

### 50 — Final Approval
Treat this as a proposed final execution. Determine whether any visible issue materially undermines communication, craft, consistency, or production readiness. Separate essential corrections from optional refinements and avoid suggesting changes simply for the sake of change.
